# Copyleft, AI authorship, and public distribution — primary-source review

Review date: 10-15-2026 (America/New_York). Jurisdictional focus: United States. Policy intent: the owner wants **all public original work copyleft**; preserve the currently selected `AGPL-3.0-only` license. This note is a risk analysis, not a legal opinion, legal warranty, or a substitute for specific counsel.

## High-confidence findings

1. **AI assistance does not eliminate copyright automatically.** The U.S. Copyright Office's 01-29-2025 report says protection may attach where a human determines sufficient expressive elements, including perceptible human-authored content, creative arrangements, and human revisions. Pure AI-generated expression with no sufficient human authorship is not protected under U.S. copyright law. Prompts alone do not automatically make their outputs protected. Evaluate expression and meaningful human choices on actual files rather than assuming either extreme.
2. **Copyleft does not create an exclusive right from nothing.** GNU explicitly states that GPL/AGPL enforcement rests on copyright held by the relevant rights holder; portions without copyrightable human authorship cannot be restricted by AGPL solely by adding its notice. AGPL remains an appropriate prospective expression of licensing intent for covered, owned material.
3. **The same work can contain differently situated portions.** Human-written/creatively modified parts, unprotectable AI-only portions, third-party works, and public-domain elements cannot all be treated identically for ownership or relicensing. Preserve notices and provenance. This repository's `Copyright 2026 Justin` statement is a claim concerning copyrightable owned contributions, **not a guarantee that every generated byte is protected or authored by him**.
4. **AGPL covers the covered work, not all adjacent work.** Reading or following a public guidebook does not mechanically turn unrelated applications, independent AI outputs, or other repositories into derivative works under AGPL. Copying copyrightable guidebook text verbatim into a distributed repository may have obligations; assess those copies separately.
5. **AGPL network-source obligations are conditional.** AGPLv3 section 13 applies to a modified version of a covered program that supports remote network interaction; it does not imply every project, website, private edit, or LLM request automatically requires public source disclosure. The corresponding-source duty depends on scope and triggering conduct.
6. **Documentation has license tradeoffs.** The FSF says GPL can license non-software work, while recommending GFDL for standalone manuals. That is a consideration, **not a finding that this repo's existing AGPL is invalid**. No relicensing or dual licensing is performed; owner intent is to retain copyleft.
7. **No prohibition on commercial usage.** OSI open-source criteria permit commercial use and redistribution. Copyleft protects recipients' rights on covered works in particular circumstances; it does not prohibit corporations from using licensed work.
8. **License cannot grant unowned rights.** The repository should not purport to grant permission for independently copyrighted third-party material. Review inbound contributions, quotations, dependency obligations, generated code whose origin is uncertain, and artwork/media separately before committing or releasing.

## Court-level confirmation and its limits

The U.S. Court of Appeals for the D.C. Circuit affirmed the Copyright Office's denial of registration for an entirely machine-authored work on **03-18-2025** in *Thaler v. Perlmutter*. The U.S. Supreme Court's official docket (No. 25-449) records that review was **denied on 03-02-2026**. A denial of certiorari is **not** a Supreme Court ruling on the legal merits; the lower-court decision remains in place. The case did not settle the factual degree of human contribution needed to copyright every particular AI-assisted computer program or document.

Primary source: Supreme Court of the United States — 03-02-2026 — https://www.supremecourt.gov/docket/docketfiles/html/public/25-449.html

## Project-specific implementation and legal boundary

- Keep `LICENSE` byte-for-byte unchanged as `AGPL-3.0-only`, keep SPDX notices in applicable original code, and preserve attribution and upstream rights.
- The public policy is not a private-data-release authorization or an automated relicensing instruction. Do not import a private chat, employer/customer data, contact identifiers, account credentials, or private repository code into public source.
- When a model is told to produce an open-source work, require verified rights and a compatible existing license before incorporation; do not treat generation by itself as evidence of originality or rights clearance.
- Keep a simple non-sensitive contributions record: relevant human revisions and decisions, known licensed source inputs, source version and the rationale for combining them. Avoid storing secret prompts/chats just to establish provenance.
- If a provenance question cannot be answered, classify the relevant asset `rights not verified` and do not label it legally cleared.
- Maintaining a public repository and passing automated checks does not validate the legal chain of title or prove AGPL enforceability against a particular downstream party.

## Adversarial legal cases

| Case | Required classification |
| --- | --- |
| Pure AI output with no qualifying human original expression | No automatic copyright or enforceable copyleft solely from a license notice |
| Human creatively rewrites or arranges AI output | Potential copyright only in qualifying human-authored expression; fact-specific |
| A third-party license governs incorporated code | Preserve its actual license and compatibility obligations; do not claim sole ownership |
| Independent app reads/follows guidebook | No automatic derivative-work status or viral relicensing |
| Another repo ships a verbatim guidebook snapshot | Review covered copying and attribution/distribution obligations, separately from its unrelated code |
| Hosted modified AGPL server app interacts with remote users | Check actual AGPL section 13 applicability to modified covered program |
| A company uses the guidebook commercially | Commercial use is allowed by open-source definition, subject to applicable license terms |
| Security/CI tests pass | No inference of verified copyright or legal compliance |
